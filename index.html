01
Foundations

How Meta's algorithm actually works

You are not buying ads. You are buying an optimization service, and it only works if you feed it enough signal.

  • The auction: every impression is auctioned. Meta shows your ad to the people it predicts will fire your conversion event.
  • The learning phase: each ad set needs roughly 50 conversion events per week to exit "Learning". Below that: Learning limited, unstable delivery, inflated CPMs, unreliable results.
  • Signal pools at the ad set level. Three ad sets at $200/day learn three times slower than one at $600/day. Same money, worse outcome.
  • The optimization event defines everything. Too rare and the algorithm starves; too shallow and it fills your CRM with junk. Choosing it is the single most important buying decision.
Golden rule

Volume of signal beats granularity of control. Every extra ad set or campaign created "to stay organized" taxes the algorithm's ability to learn.

02
SOP · Account structure

Structure: as flat as possible

Campaign = one funnel / offer→Ad set = one audience→Ads = concepts being tested
  • One campaign per funnel. Never mix funnels in one ad set: Meta won't balance them, it shifts all spend to whichever produces the cheapest event and silently starves the other.
  • 1–2 ad sets per campaign, max. Broad targeting as default. New ad set only when the audience or funnel is genuinely different, never to organize creatives.
  • New ad goes into an existing ad set by default. That's the habit that keeps accounts healthy.
  • Testing → core promotion: a testing campaign validates new concepts on a capped budget; winners get promoted into the core campaign where spend scales behind them.
  • Broad beats hyper-targeting. Meta can't target firmographics reliably anyway. The copy is the targeting.
Common failure mode

40 ad sets, most paused, actives fragmented at $50–200/day, everything Learning limited. Consolidate first, then judge performance. You cannot evaluate creative inside a starving structure.

03
SOP · Creative testing

The concept system: one ad, one argument

The atomic unit of testing is a numbered concept (we call them DCTs): one angle, one argument, expressed as a set of hooks plus one body.

  • One value proposition per ad. An ad that argues three things argues nothing.
  • Naming convention: DCT_017_BRAND_Concept Name. The number follows one sequence per client and is carried into UTMs, reports and the creative library, so every result traces back to its concept.
  • Volume per concept: ~5 hooks for static, ~3 for video/UGC, one universal body. Hooks are the variable; the argument is the constant.
  • The angle library compounds: winners' verbatim on-creative copy seeds the next generation; losers document which arguments the market rejected.
The hook specificity test · before and after writing every hook

"This ad runs direct response to a broad audience. The copy is the only targeting. Is this hook specific and pre-qualifying enough that only the ideal customer identifies with it, or could any other niche fit into it?" If no, rewrite until yes.

04
SOP · Copy

Copy rules that survived reality

Ground hooks in real voice-of-customer

Pull the actual language buyers use (forums, sales calls, replies) and use the closest verbatim match. The pain being real is not enough; the wording must mirror how the buyer says it. If the VOC isn't visceral enough, collect more VOC, don't invent it.

Body structure that converts

Agitate (1–2 lines)→Bridge / mechanism→Specific proof→One CTA

Disqualify in the copy

When the offer has a floor (minimum budget, company size), say so in the ad. A disqualifier filters clicks you'd pay for and never convert. Quantify the client's effort, guarantee only what you control.

Ban list

No "game-changer / unlock / transform / revolutionize". No hype adjectives carrying the argument. Simple, native-feeling creative consistently beats polished brand-looking ads in feed.

05
Framework · Metrics

Two metrics, in strict order

Every funnel has a volume metric (cost per lead) and a quality metric (cost per qualified lead / booked call / verified subscriber). Ranking creatives requires both, weighted.

MetricRoleWeight
Volume CAC (CPL)Primary ranking factor: can it acquire at target cost?~70%
Quality CAC (CPQL / cost per call)Tiebreaker and guardrail: are the leads real?~30%
  • Quality tolerance: quality CAC up to ~20–30% above target is acceptable only if volume CAC is on target.
  • CTR, CPM, CVR are diagnostics, not decision metrics. CTR is lumpy at weekly volumes: use it analytically, never rank by it.
  • Minimum sample: filter out creatives with fewer than ~5 quality conversions before ranking. Ranking on 2 conversions is ranking on noise.
06
SOP · Scaling decisions

The scaling decision matrix

ConditionAction
Volume CAC ≤ target and quality CAC ≤ ~130% of targetScale
Volume CAC ≤ target, quality far above targetReduce spend, investigate quality
Volume CAC above target, quality exceptionalHold (never scale), note tradeoff
Volume CAC above targetCut or hold. Never scale.
The non-negotiable

Never scale a creative whose volume CAC is above target, even with excellent quality CAC. Scaling it pulls the whole account average further above target.

Tiebreakers (volume CAC within ~10%)

  • Both above target: the one with meaningfully better quality CAC (>30% lower) and higher CTR earns the budget.
  • Both on target and near-identical: don't chase a marginal ~8% volume edge if its quality CAC is ~2× worse. Quality breaks the tie.

Pause vs reduce

Never pause on one bad week. Look at 3–4 weeks plus all-time. Sustained off-target → pause. Only 1–2 weeks off, or thin history → reduce and monitor.

07
SOP · Budget

Budget pacing: a ritual, not a vibe

  1. Each account has a monthly budget. Confirm it at the start of every month, budgets change.
  2. Weekly, compute month-to-date spend vs where you should be: "Day 12 of 31 = 38.7% through the month."
  3. Label each account over / under / on pace.
  4. Adjustment = (remaining budget ÷ days left) vs current daily budget. Over pace → reduce. Under pace → increase behind proven winners only. On pace → say so and leave it alone.
Scaling mechanics

Raise budgets in steps of ~20–30% every few days, large jumps reset learning. Scale by adding budget to winning ad sets, not by cloning them into duplicates (duplicates fragment signal and compete in the same auctions).

08
Tracking · Pixel & events

Pixel, CAPI, and choosing the event

  • Pixel = browser-side. Conversions API = server-side. Run both, deduplicated: browsers block more every year, CAPI recovers what the pixel misses.
  • Map the whole funnel as events: page view → engagement → quiz start → quiz complete → lead → booked call. Optimize on one, measure all.
  • Verify what each event fires on. Real case: a report used complete_registration (fired on a form step) instead of lead (the actual subscription): CPL read $9.01 when the truth was $3.27. Confirm the mapping in Events Manager before choosing your denominator.
  • Zero conversions on a live account is a tracking bug until proven otherwise. Check the pixel before concluding "the ads don't work."
Choosing the optimization event

Pick the deepest event that still clears ~50 fires/week per ad set. If booked calls run 10/month, optimizing on them starves the algorithm: optimize on the lead and feed call quality back through the CRM.

09
Tracking · CRM loop

Close the loop: CRM → Meta

The endgame of B2B media buying: Meta optimizing for leads that become revenue, without switching to a sparse event.

  1. Connect the CRM to Meta (HubSpot has a native integration: Marketing → Ads → connect the account, enable CRM event syncing).
  2. Keep lifecycle stages honest: lead → qualified → call booked → won. This progression is the training data.
  3. Switch to Conversion Leads optimization once a few hundred synced leads with stage progression accrue. Meta keeps optimizing on lead volume but biases delivery toward profiles that historically progress.
  4. Expectation: pays off as data accrues, not in week one. Connect early precisely because of this.

Same principle for newsletters: subscriber quality (opens, verified status) fed back per-concept shows which angle brings readers vs. dead emails.

10
Tracking · UTMs

UTMs: the attribution spine

Meta reports what Meta saw. Your CRM reports what happened. UTMs are the join key, and they only work if the schema is rigid.

ParameterConventionWhy
utm_sourceFixed per channel: facebookadsSplits paid from organic everywhere downstream
utm_mediumAd set nameAudience-level rollups
utm_campaignConcept name (DCT_017_...)Revenue per angle: feeds the angle library
utm_contentMeta ad ID ({{ad.id}})Unambiguous join to the exact ad
utm_termAd set ID ({{adset.id}})Same, at ad set level
  • Use Meta's dynamic URL parameters so tagging is automatic and typo-free.
  • A broken UTM is silent data loss. Audit UTMs whenever a creative shows spend with zero attributed conversions, it's usually the link, not the ad.
  • Belt and suspenders: capture the Meta click ID (fbclid) into the CRM too. Paid attribution = UTM match or click ID present.
11
Tracking · Attribution

When numbers disagree (they will)

  • Meta counts by attribution window (default 7-day click, 1-day view); your platform counts by UTM at signup. They never match exactly. Both are "right" under their own definition.
  • Spend always comes from the ad platform. Never take a composite cost metric (CPL, CPQL) from a dashboard you haven't reconciled against platform spend.
  • Real case: an internal dashboard read cost-per-qualified-lead at $15.70 while Meta showed $49.98, missing campaigns in the spend column plus mismatched UTMs. It even generated a false "kill" recommendation for the creative driving all qualified leads. Fix: recompute from source of truth (Meta spend ÷ CRM-scored qualified leads) before acting.
  • Before any kill/scale decision based on a dashboard: reconcile it against Meta once. Material deltas mean the dashboard is broken, not the creative.
Rule

Never pause a creative because one system shows zero conversions. First check: UTM intact? Event firing? Attribution window? Most "dead" creatives are tracking bugs.

12
Downstream · Lead hygiene

Buying doesn't end at the click

Every dollar of paid acquisition is wasted if the lead lands in a dirty database or the follow-up email lands in spam. Buying and deliverability are one system.

  • Validate at the door: email validation on forms, double opt-in on lists. Gated content without validation fills your list with asdf@gmail.com.
  • Never bulk-import cold contacts (purchased lists, full CRM exports) into a sending platform. Fastest way to destroy a sending domain; recovery takes months of disciplined engaged-only sending.
  • Sunset non-engagers automatically: no opens/clicks in 90–120 days → suppress. Providers rank you by engagement rate; dead weight taxes every future send.
  • Authenticate the domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC with reports) and monitor via Google Postmaster. Authentication buys the right to compete for the inbox; engagement wins it.
  • Nurture only works on leads that expected email. Consent at capture is a deliverability strategy, not just a legal one.
13
SOP · Reporting

Weekly report: decisions, not dashboards

  1. Budget pacing: month, day, % through, each account over/under/on pace.
  2. Cost analysis: volume CAC first, quality CAC second, each marked on/off target. Always that order.
  3. Top 3 creatives ranked by volume + volume CAC (quality as context), with preview links.
  4. Winning angle: one paragraph on why the winner wins, the argument, not the asset.
  5. Next steps: the most important section. Every bullet an action: scale / hold / reduce / pause / investigate.
  • Week-over-week deltas are mandatory for every KPI. A number without its delta is trivia.
  • Log the numbers after every report (spend, leads, CPL, quality, top creatives, and the source of prior-week figures). Next week's WoW depends on this ritual.
  • Secondary KPIs (CPM, CTR, CVR) never go ahead of the decision metrics.
14
Recap

The ten golden rules

  1. Signal volume beats structure granularity. 1–2 ad sets per campaign.
  2. One ad, one argument. The copy is the targeting.
  3. Optimize on the deepest event clearing ~50/week; feed deeper quality via CRM.
  4. Volume CAC decides, quality CAC vetoes. Never scale above-target volume CAC.
  5. Never pause on one bad week. 3–4 week pattern or reduce-and-monitor.
  6. Rigid UTM schema + dynamic parameters. Concept in campaign slot, ad ID in content.
  7. Spend from the ad platform, always. Reconcile dashboards before acting.
  8. Zero conversions = tracking bug until proven otherwise.
  9. Ground copy in verbatim customer language. Winners seed the next test.
  10. Protect the destination: validated capture, double opt-in, engaged-only sending, authenticated domain.
