01
Foundations

The learning phase

Every ad set needs about 50 conversion events a week to get out of the learning phase.

While an ad set is in learning, Meta is still figuring out who to show the ad to. An ad set stuck in the learning phase will always buy conversions at a higher cost than an ad set that made it out.

Changes reset learning

  • Any meaningful change (budget, creative, targeting) resets the campaign. Learning restarts from zero and Meta discards the data it had already gathered.
  • This is how accounts get stuck: in learning, CPL is always high. The high CPL tempts you into another change, which resets learning again, and the cycle keeps going. At that point the account lives in the learning phase permanently.
  • Give every change a minimum of 72 hours before judging it or touching anything else.
  • Change one thing at a time. When you change many things at once, you never know which change worked.

SOP · Account structure

The structure I recommend

Campaign = one funnel→Ad set = one audience→Ad = 3–5 variations of one angle
  • One funnel per campaign. In practice that means one URL per campaign.
  • One audience per ad set.
  • 3 to 5 variations of the same angle per ad.

SOP · Concepts

One ad is actually a concept

One ad set contains multiple ads. Each ad is really a concept: a group of variations of the same ad.

I usually run CBO with dynamic creative on, so Meta moves the spend to whichever variation is working best.

CBO keeps it simple and easy to optimize and control CPL. We have some clients on ABO, but CBO is better most of the time.

SOP · Budget rules

Budget vs number of ads

  • I usually set a daily budget minimum of $5 on each ad set. It makes sure Meta gives every ad set enough budget to actually be tested.
  • Always be mindful of the budget vs the number of ads. Divide the budget by the number of ads: each ad should get at least 10 conversions a day worth of spend. If it doesn't, you have too many ads or too little budget.
Cost cap

Cost cap works when the budget is low. At $500 it will not be able to find cheap leads, because what you are telling Meta is "find leads at only this cost". When spend is high and the audience is narrow, it will not spend the full budget.

05
SOP · Daily routine

The daily check, in ten minutes

  • Open the month-to-date view, all campaigns. Last month is done, you can't fix it, so don't stare at it.
  • Compare yesterday against the month-to-date baseline. An ad set that runs $30 CPL all month and hit $70 yesterday is telling you something.
  • When one goes sideways: cut its budget (say $700/day → $400), let it earn its way back, then restore. And reallocate the difference the same day, to whichever ad set has been boringly consistent all month. The daily total gets spent either way; you choose where.
  • Once a week, scan the week-over-week deltas on CPM, CTR, conversion rate and cost per result. A conversion rate that fell from 29% to 8% has a cause. Drill from account to campaign to ad until you find where the drop is coming from. You won't always find the cause, but you'll know where to look next.
  • For ads where quality data arrives late (sales, activations, call outcomes): cap their spend (say $30/day) until the quality verdict is in. Don't let an unproven ad spend big just because its CPL looks good.
06
Craft · Audience plays

Getting more out of broad targeting

  • Exclude past converters properly. Uploading the email list barely works, the email someone subscribes with rarely matches the one on their decade-old Facebook account. Build a website custom audience of everyone who converted in the last 180 days and exclude that. Meta knows exactly who they are.
  • Use breakdowns to discover the real ICP. Break results down by age, gender, day. Sometimes the client's assumed customer and the converting customer are different people, that's a finding worth bringing to them. Look at conversion rate, not just volume, and don't read anything into $200 of spend.
  • Bias with value rules, don't narrow the targeting. If the 45–54 bracket converts best, don't restrict the ad set to it. Set a value rule: "bid 20% more for 45–54". You keep the broad setup that works and still push budget where the data points. Works for gender, days, even hours.
  • Time retargeting to the funnel's natural rhythm. If subscribers typically buy the follow-up offer around week three, retarget them in that window with that offer, not on day one with a generic reminder.
  • Watch frequency for fatigue. Above ~2 on active ads (measured since launch, per ad set) means people are seeing it too often. Pair it with CTR: frequency climbing while CTR falls is the fatigue signature.
07
Tracking · Pixel & events

Pixel, CAPI, and picking the right event

  • Run both trackers: the pixel in the browser and the Conversions API from the server, deduplicated. Browsers block more tracking every year; CAPI catches what the pixel drops. Most tools (HubSpot, Zapier, most ESPs) do this without custom code.
  • Map the whole journey as events: page view, content engagement, quiz start, quiz complete, lead, booked call. You'll optimize on one but you want eyes on all of them.
  • Then verify what each event actually fires on. A report once used the "registration" event as the lead count and showed a $9.01 CPL; the real lead event said $3.27. Same account, same week, one wrong assumption about an event name.
  • And if a live account shows zero conversions, that's a tracking bug until you've proven otherwise. Check the pixel before concluding the ads don't work.
Picking the optimization event

Go as deep in the funnel as you can while still clearing ~50 events a week per ad set. Reality check: a booked call costs around $300 on average, so below roughly $10k/month in spend, a call-optimized funnel simply never gets enough events to learn. Optimize on the lead and feed call quality back through the CRM.

08
Tracking · UTMs

Standard UTM parameters

This is the standard UTM string I use on every ad:

?utm_source=facebookads&utm_medium={{adset.name}}&utm_campaign={{ad.name}}

The dynamic parameters ({{adset.name}}, {{ad.name}}) fill themselves in, so every ad gets tagged automatically.

Naming convention

For this to work, we need one standard naming convention for collateral, for example COLL_101_AD NAME. The ad name flows straight into the UTMs, so the naming convention is what makes tracking usable.

09
Recap

The ten rules

  1. Signal volume beats neat organization. One or two ad sets per campaign.
  2. One ad, one argument. The copy is the targeting.
  3. Optimize on the deepest event that still clears ~50 a week. Feed deeper quality back through the CRM.
  4. Volume CAC decides, quality CAC vetoes. Above-target volume never scales.
  5. One bad week proves nothing. Patterns earn pauses; blips earn reductions.
  6. One UTM schema, dynamic parameters, same for everyone.
  7. Spend comes from the ad platform. Reconcile every dashboard once before trusting it.
  8. Zero conversions is a tracking bug until proven otherwise.
  9. Steal the customer's words. Winners' copy seeds the next round.
  10. Protect the destination: validated capture, double opt-in, engaged-only sending, authenticated domain.
